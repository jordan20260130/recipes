# Whole Roasted Onions

*Reconstructed from memory, 2026-09-24. 朴 remembered a very simple
method: one sweet onion, unpeeled and uncut, roasted whole, then salted
(maybe buttered). The temperature and time were the lost bits; the
timing was checked against real-world references the same day. 朴's own
ideal runs **60–75 minutes**, and since he reaches for large-ish sweet
onions, that's the range this recipe now leads with.*

## Ingredients

- 1 sweet onion — Vidalia, Walla Walla, Maui, or a good yellow onion —
  whole, **skin left on, root end intact**
- Coarse or flaky salt
- Optional: 1 tsp butter, olive oil, black pepper, a sprig of thyme

Notes on the onion: sweet varieties are the point here — and
**Vidalia / Walla Walla / Maui are 朴's favorite kind**, the ones this
recipe is tuned around. They carry more sugar and less sulfur, so they
go soft and jammy. A storage/yellow onion works but stays sharper and
more savory.

## Method

1. Heat the oven to **400°F (200°C)**, rack in the middle.
2. Rinse the onion and rub off any loose dirt. **Do not peel, cut, or
   trim.** The intact skin holds the onion together and steams the
   inside while the outside roasts.
3. Set it on a small sheet of foil, or in a small ovenproof dish /
   ramekin on a baking sheet. (The skin will blacken and may drip a
   little.)
4. Roast **60–75 minutes** — the working range for the large-ish sweet
   onions 朴 favors. (A medium onion can be done in ~45–60 minutes; a
   large one can run to ~75.) Oven range that all works: 375–425°F;
   lower = gentler and sweeter, higher = more char.
   - **Done when:** the skin is dark, papery, and blistered, and the
     onion yields completely when you press it — soft like a baked
     potato. A skewer should slide through with no resistance.
5. Rest 5–10 minutes, until cool enough to handle.
6. Peel away the charred outer skin, or cut off the root end and
   squeeze / scoop the soft interior out.
7. Season with salt. If using butter, put the pat on the hot onion so it
   melts in. Pepper and thyme to taste.

## On the caramelization

An unpeeled, whole onion mostly *steams* under its own skin, so you get a
very sweet, soft, golden-to-jammy interior rather than the deep brown of
a sauté pan. The layers just under the skin do brown, and the outside
goes dark and caramelized — so "slightly caramelized" fits the outer
edge, less so the middle.

If you want real caramelized edges: halve the onion and roast it
cut-side down (or finish cut-side down under the broiler for a few
minutes). That said, the whole-skinned version is the one that matches
the remembered method.

## Notes & Variations

- Smaller onions (shallots, pearl onions) roast much faster — roughly
  25–35 minutes.
- More savory: add a splash of balsamic, soy, or stock to the dish.
  Butter + thyme + a little white wine is the classic pairing.
- Leftovers: good chopped into soups, on toast with cheese, or mashed
  into butter.

## Food Safety

Treat as any cooked food: refrigerate within 2 hours, and eat within
about 4 days.
